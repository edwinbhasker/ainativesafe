🔄 Human-AI Collaboration Patterns in Banking

🔴 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

  • AI pauses at defined decision points; human approves before proceeding.
  • Use for credit decisions, AML triage, customer-facing advice and material decisions.

🟡 Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL)

  • AI operates within guardrails; humans monitor dashboards and exceptions.
  • Use for transaction monitoring, fraud patterns, dependencies and forecasting.

🟢 Human-out-of-the-Loop (HOOTL)

  • Full autonomy for predetermined low-risk scenarios with audit trails.
  • Use for code linting, documentation, internal FAQs and log analysis.

🔵 Co-Creative Collaboration

  • Humans and AI jointly contribute cognitive value.
  • Use for product innovation, scenario testing, process redesign and strategy.
Decision Rights: AI can recommend across all tiers. AI may execute only within defined guardrails for Tier-2 and Tier-3. Humans retain final authority for Tier-1 material decisions affecting customers, capital or regulatory reporting.
🏛️ Banking AI Governance Architecture — SR 26-2 Aligned

1. Approved-Use Boundary

  • Define prohibited autonomous uses in credit, capital estimation and regulatory filing.
  • Map every agent to intended use, data scope and decision boundary.

2. Evidence & Quality

  • Anchor outputs to approved institutional evidence.
  • Track groundedness and human override rationales.

3. Output Monitoring

  • Track accuracy, completeness, hallucination and drift.
  • Maintain prompt → context → response → action → outcome audit trails.

4. Third-Party AI Governance

  • Bring vendor AI into governance; define audit rights, residency and fallback procedures.

🗺️ 90-Day Implementation Roadmap
Phase 1: Foundation & Inventory (Days 1–30)
Complete AI inventory; establish AI Governance Committee; update MRM policy; launch AI literacy training; deploy regulatory scanning.
Phase 2: Pilot ART & Agent Deployment (Days 31–60)
Select a non-material value stream; deploy DevEx and Quality Guard agents in supervised mode; run AI-Augmented PI Planning; establish audit logging.
Phase 3: Scale & Govern (Days 61–90)
Expand to material value streams with HITL; deploy dependency orchestration; conduct independent effective challenge; establish outcome-based funding.
Phase 4: Optimize & Transform (Days 91–180)
Restructure teams around human-AI capability pairs; implement continuous monitoring; measure cycle-time compression; prepare regulatory evidence.
Critical Success Factor: Do not declare victory after buying copilots. Look for measurable product impact, curated context, explicit agent boundaries, variable-cost visibility and teams that respond to evidence.
