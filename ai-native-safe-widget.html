🔄 Human-AI Collaboration Patterns in Banking

🔴 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

  • Pattern: AI pauses at defined decision points; human must approve before proceeding
  • Banking Use: Credit decisions, AML triage, customer-facing advice, adverse-action language, model validation reports
  • Quote: "There is no customer-facing use case affecting our decisions without a human in the loop." — Truist Bank
  • Staffing: Dedicated reviewers per active AI workflow

🟡 Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL)

  • Pattern: AI operates autonomously within guardrails; humans monitor dashboards and intervene on exceptions
  • Banking Use: Transaction monitoring, fraud pattern detection, dependency management, capacity forecasting, internal research
  • Guardrails: Confidence thresholds, materiality limits, four-eyes rules on writes above threshold
  • Staffing: Supervisors monitor multiple AI agents simultaneously

🟢 Human-out-of-the-Loop (HOOTL)

  • Pattern: Full autonomy for predetermined low-risk scenarios; humans review post-operationally
  • Banking Use: Code linting, documentation generation, internal FAQ handling, log analysis, non-material reporting
  • Requirement: Audit trail of every autonomous action; periodic sampling review
  • Staffing: Periodic auditors, not real-time oversight

🔵 Co-Creative Collaboration

  • Pattern: Humans and AI jointly contribute cognitive value; neither could produce outcome alone
  • Banking Use: Product innovation workshops, scenario stress testing, process redesign, strategic planning
  • Key: Shared authority, context-dependent; requires high-trust teams and mature AI literacy
Decision Rights Framework: AI can recommend across all tiers. AI may execute only within defined guardrails for Tier-2 and Tier-3. Humans retain final authority for all Tier-1 (material) decisions affecting customers, capital, or regulatory reporting.

🏛️ Banking AI Governance Architecture — SR 26-2 Aligned

1. Approved-Use Boundary Determination

  • Prevent GenAI from autonomous roles in credit scoring, capital estimation, or regulatory filing without HITL
  • Define "materiality tiers" per SR 26-2: Tier-1 (material), Tier-2 (standard), Tier-3 (low)
  • Map every AI agent to its intended use, data scope, and decision boundary

2. Evidence-Grounded Sufficiency & Quality

  • All AI outputs anchored to approved, relevant institutional evidence
  • Groundedness scoring for RAG-based agents; citation accuracy checks
  • Human override rationales logged for every challenge to AI output

3. Output Evaluation & Monitoring

  • Accuracy, completeness, relevance, hallucination rate tracked continuously
  • Guardrail trigger rate monitored; drift detection on model behavior
  • Full audit trail: prompt → context → response → human action → outcome

4. Third-Party & Vendor AI Governance

  • Vendor AI models brought into MRM program — SOC 2 alone is insufficient
  • Contractual audit rights, data residency, and model card requirements
  • Fallback procedures when vendor APIs are unavailable or drift

🗺️ 90-Day Implementation Roadmap — Banking AI-Native SAFe
Phase 1: Foundation & Inventory (Days 1–30)
• Complete honest AI inventory: every model, agent, LLM endpoint, and third-party SaaS with embedded AI
• Establish AI Governance Committee with CRO, CCO, CMRO, CIO, and business line leaders
• Update MRM policy to explicitly include GenAI and agentic systems; define materiality tiers
• Launch AI-Empowered Leading SAFe training for executives and AI-Native Foundations for practitioners
• Deploy Regulatory Horizon Scanner agent for continuous compliance monitoring
Phase 2: Pilot ART & Agent Deployment (Days 31–60)
• Select one non-material value stream (e.g., internal operations or HR) for first AI-Augmented ART
• Deploy DevEx Agent in supervised mode; establish human validation gates
• Implement Quality & Compliance Guard Agent with auto-blocking for security violations
• Run first AI-Augmented PI Planning with PI Planning Co-Pilot; measure time savings and alignment quality
• Establish audit logging in bank's SIEM for all AI interactions; identity-bind to IdP
Phase 3: Scale & Govern (Days 61–90)
• Expand to material value streams (consumer lending, AML) with full HITL controls
• Deploy Dependency Orchestrator across Solution Trains; integrate with existing ALM toolchain
• Conduct first "Effective Challenge" review of AI agents by independent MRM validators
• Establish outcome-based funding: connect every AI investment to measurable business outcomes
• Launch AI Value Architect role formally across all ARTs; define outcome trees for top 5 value streams
Phase 4: Optimize & Transform (Days 91–180)
• Transition from AI-Empowered to AI-Native: restructure teams around human-AI capability pairs
• Implement continuous monitoring dashboards for agent performance, drift, and hallucination rates
• Achieve 15–30% cycle time compression in pilot ARTs; document and replicate patterns
• Prepare for regulatory examination with complete audit trails, model cards, and lineage documentation
• Host first enterprise "AI-Native Inspect & Adapt" — measure transformation impact, recalibrate
Critical Success Factor: Do not declare victory after buying copilots. Look for a working outcome tree, curated context, measurable product impact, explicit agent boundaries, variable-cost visibility, and teams that can sense evidence and respond without waiting for a long approval chain.
